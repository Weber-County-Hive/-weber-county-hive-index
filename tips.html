How we handle tips

  • Private tips (Messenger, email, the tip form): the sender's name is never published, on this page or anywhere else.
  • Public comments and posts (Facebook and similar): shown with first and last initials only, when a name is available.
  • Your information stays yours. We don't use personal information from a tip unless you tell us we may.
  • Every tip is checked against public records: filings, bills, budgets, minutes and official notices.
  • We publish what the records confirm, label what they don't, and say when something is still being checked.
  • A tip is not a finding. Nothing goes into a case file until a record backs it.

"A tax increase is a tax increase… I would also vote yes on requiring the same for legislators."

Right on the recordElections & ballot measures
  • B's 60% rule applies to a tax increase in an initiative whoever pays it, businesses included.
  • Legislators pass taxes by a majority of members elected (Art. VI, Sec. 22); no 60% rule for them is on the ballot.

Oct. 7, 2026 · Public comment on a Hive graphic

"YES on B should say 'tax increase,' not 'tax change.' NO on B should say the Legislature can only amend initiatives that do not alter or reform government."

Partly verified · we corrected our graphicElections & ballot measures
  • Right: "tax change" was too broad, since plain tax cuts still need a simple majority. The graphic now says "tax increase, as S.J.R. 2 defines it."
  • Partly right: the Utah Supreme Court (July 11, 2024) said the Legislature can still amend a government-reform initiative if the change doesn't impair the reform, and a change that impairs it must be narrowly tailored to a compelling government interest. The ruling doesn't limit amending other initiatives. Our NO column now names that limit.
